# Beam-to-face scope and source audit

ISO 6336-1:2019, PDF page 65 (printed 57), 7.5.3.5.1–.2: f_sh includes pinion and shaft bending and torsion. The approximate bending component uses shaft deflection slope at the face center times face width, with force assumed at the center. Its torsion approximation assumes load distributed evenly over the face and a solid cylinder of diameter d1; equations 59–60 also contain an empirical term and material assumptions. A simple endpoint deflection difference is not equation 59 or full f_sh. PDF page 55 (printed 47), 7.4.3.2–.3, describes face increments and gear-body bending and torsion; Method B requires additional compliance and load distribution modeling.

`gearmath.gear.beam_face.face_components` provides only an Euler–Bernoulli center-slope bending contribution and adds a caller-supplied, independently obtained torsional displacement difference. Both are signed high-x minus low-x, projected onto the specified unit direction in the y–z plane; coordinates and displacement are mm, angle is degrees from +y toward +z. The center must be a beam solve node, face endpoints lie inside the modeled shaft, and the caller must explicitly acknowledge omitted gear-body, housing, bearing compliance and wheel contributions. No f_sh or KHbeta is produced and nothing is coupled to the rating pipeline. A caller must ensure its externally calculated torsion has that same sign and projection and does not contain wheel or other separately applied deflections. Zero torsion is permitted only as an explicit supplied result with its source.

Limitations: the beam has rigid point supports, lacks distributed torque and gear-body/housing compliance, and its Timoshenko section rotation is not the deflection slope. Deriving torsion internally needs a distributed mesh-torque model, gear geometry and contact-direction projection, plus a verified source-specific sign and compliance convention; a complete ISO equivalent misalignment also needs the relevant gear/body/support effects and the prescribed combination and running-in treatment. This partial result must not be represented as Method B or the complete equation 59 result. Tests check a simply supported point-loaded Euler–Bernoulli beam against its independent closed-form signed slope and add an explicit torsion example; they are not an ISO worked example or validation of the external torsion model.
